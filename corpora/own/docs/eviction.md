# Eviction

Each week begins with the crowning of the Head of Household (HOH). The HOH’s primary responsibility is to nominate two houseguests for eviction — often referred to as putting them “on the block.” Certain twists may result in a third nominee being placed on the chopping block.

The nominees, HOH, and three other randomly selected players then compete in the Power of Veto (POV) competition. The POV winner can either “save” a nominee from the block or choose not to use it. If the Veto is used, the HOH must immediately name a replacement nominee, unless the nominee saved was a special third nominee, who typically has no replacement. Once the Veto meeting is over, nominations are considered final. These final nominees will face the vote to evict during the live eviction episode.

The Live Eviction episode, which typically airs on Thursday nights, is the end of the week. The final nominees are given a chance to make a brief final pleas to the houseguests. These pleas are often used to show loyalty, make last-minute deals, or call out a rival.

After both nominees speak, the rest of the eligible voting houseguests head one-by-one into the Diary Room to cast their vote to evict. This vote is done secretly so no one can be influenced in real time. The host, Julie Chen-Moonves or Arisa Cox, privately hears each vote.

The HOH and the nominees cannot vote (except in a tie where the HOH publicly casts the deciding vote). The host tallies the votes, evicts the houseguest with the majority, and announces, “By a vote of X to Y… [Name], you are evicted from the Big Brother House.” The evicted houseguest has about one minute to gather their belongings, say goodbye, and walk out the front door. Once on stage, the evicted houseguest sits down with Julie (or Arisa) for their exit interview before they are either heading home or, if it is during jury phase, traveling to the jury house. Evicted players during this stage often see “goodbye messages” prerecorded by their fellow houseguests.

From season 2 through season 9, eviction votes were often prerecorded in advance, with the results revealed during the live eviction episode. Live voting became the standard starting with season 10, with the addition of a live studio audience.

After a houseguest is evicted, their photo on the Memory Wall turns black-and-white. Most of the time, eviction is permanent, but there may be rare occasions where a houseguest re-enters the house by competition or the voting of fellow houseguests.

Other instances of short-term returns include Jase Wirey returning in a Grim Reaper costume in season 7, Jessie Godderz returning in a gorilla costume in season 10, Casey Turner returning to host the OTEV Power of Veto competition in season 11 (although he had no direct contact with the houseguests, communicating the rules of the game through the PA system), and Rachel Reilly returning for 24 hours as a result of Pandora's Box in season 12. During Week 12 of season 16, the jury members - Jocasta Odom, Hayden Voss, Zach Rance, Donny Thompson, Nicole Franzel and Christine Brecht returned to the house to compete in a Luxury Competition with the remaining houseguests betting on which would win. Hayden won, granting himself and Victoria Rafaeli $5,000 each. However, the remaining houseguests were isolated from the jurors.

If evicted during jury phase, players move to the Jury House, where they remain sequestered until finale night. The jury ultimately votes for the winner of the season in the Final Two (or Final Three) on the live finale.

This process is the most common eviction process used by most international Big Brother versions, where housemates nominate between themselves, and the ones with the most votes are nominated for a public vote that will determine who will be evicted. The voting is usually a week-long process, where nominations starting the week and evictions ending the week. The voting opens after the nominations take place, however before the voting lines are opened, the nominees are given few minutes to make pleas on why they deserve to stay.

Depending on the format of the season, the viewer voting has three types; either the public votes to evict (with the housemate with the most votes being evicted) or votes to save (with the housemate with the fewest votes being evicted), or a save-evict voting (a combination of the vote to save and vote to evict).

In Big Brother 1 (US) the eviction process was different from that of the following American seasons and followed the international format of Eviction. Instead, a "banishment" happened every two weeks, with all of the houseguests voting on who the nominees would be. America would then decide who would be evicted.

In Pinoy Big Brother: Lucky 7, a new and special format of weekly eviction was introduced, an Eviction Competition. Instead of a voting system, the nominees will compete in an eviction challenge every week wherein they must avoid falling in last place. The housemate who performed poorly in the challenge will be the one evicted. In Pinoy Big Brother, the eviction challenge is considered as one of the variants of the series' safety challenge, Ligtask Challenge.

Due to Pinoy Big Brother: Lucky 7's season format, the Eviction Ligtask Challenge was used as the season's main eviction format until the house was down to seven housemates. When only seven housemates remained, the public voting became the main eviction process and the Eviction Ligtask was replaced by the Veto Ligtask. This format was used in all three parts of the four-part season. The Eviction challenge also made recurring appearances as a special twist in the succeeding seasons.

Hit The Road in Big Brother (U.S.) is considered a variant of an eviction competition, but it was used only as a one-time twist per season, not a main format for evicting contestants. It became the main format of the spin off season Big Brother: Reindeer Games, where houseguests were referred to as "eliminated" upon their exits instead of "evicted".

In some cases, houseguests must be removed from the house, or ejected. This can be for many reasons. They can be ejected for violating rules of the house, or eliminated from the house via competition. In other rare situations, a houseguest chooses to walk from the game. This is when a houseguest goes to the diary room and self evicts.

In some cases, eviction processes are affected by twists.

Fake Eviction: A houseguest is evicted but isn't actually out of the game. Instead, the fake evicted houseguest is usually sent in a secret location in relation to a bigger twist.
Double Eviction: Two houseguests are evicted in one day, or within the same week.
Triple Eviction: Three houseguests are evicted in one day or within the same week.
Mass Eviction: Four or more houseguests are evicted in one day or within the same week.
Instant Eviction: A houseguest is evicted immediately in one day.
Elimination: A houseguest is removed from the house after losing a competition.
Surprise Eviction: A houseguest may be evicted by surprise. They can be evicted any time of the day.
Accelerated Eviction: A week before the final, succeeding evictions may take place per day, determining the finalists of the season from the last eviction.

Martin Jonkman from Big Brother 1 (Netherlands) is the first-ever housemate to get evicted in the entire series of Big Brother.
Big Brother 1 (US) is the only season in Big Brother history that didn't use the term "eviction" or "evicted", instead they used "banishment" or "banished".
Big Brother 2 (US) is the first season to adopt the North American format where contestants evict each other, taking the eviction power away from the viewers.

Only five players have appeared in multiple seasons of Big Brother US and Big Brother Canada and have never been evicted.
Dick Donato won Big Brother 8 (US) and walked during Week 1 of Big Brother 13 (US).
Dan Gheesling won Big Brother 10 and made the Final 2 again in Big Brother 14 (US).
Paul Abrahamian made the Final 2 in both Big Brother 18 (US) and Big Brother 19 (US).
Cody Calafiore was the runner-up of Big Brother 16 (US) and won Big Brother 22 (US)'.
Anthony Douglas made the Final 2 and was the runner-up in both Big Brother Canada 7 and Big Brother Canada 12.
Dan, Paul, Cody and Anthony are the only three to never be eliminated overall.
Victor Arroyo is the only person to be evicted 3 times in a single season.
Brendon Villegas, Sindy Nguyen and Nicole Franzel have been evicted 3 times across multiple seasons.
Janelle Pierzina and Kaysar Ridha have both been evicted 4 times across multiple seasons.
Nicole Franzel is the only winner to have been a two-time evicted houseguest in another season.
