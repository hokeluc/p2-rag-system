# Returning Players

Returning players, also known as "returnees," are contestants who have previously competed in a season of Big Brother and then come back for another chance to compete and win. The concept of returning players has been part of the franchise since early seasons.

All-Stars seasons in Big Brother feature entire casts of returning players. These seasons are designed to make very competitive environments by bringing back fan-favorite houseguests with proven track records. The first and most infamous All-Stars season was Big Brother 7 (US), included returning contestants from the first six seasons. Another notable All-Stars season is Big Brother 22 (US).

Several Big Brother seasons have featured a blended cast of returning players competing alongside new players. For example, Big Brother Canada 5 had eight returning houseguests with eight new players. Similarly, Celebrity Big Brother 19 (UK) marked the first time returning celebrity housemates competed directly against new celebrity contestants. These mixed casts show how well returnees can use their prior experience against new players.

Several Big Brother seasons have featured a blended cast where a single returning houseguest competes alongside an otherwise new cast. Seasons have also experimented with bringing back four veterans as team captains for new players, and three duo pairs to compete against first-time players in duos. Across these formats, the returning players are usually in the minority.

Big Brother 7, the first All-Stars season, featured 20 potential returnee candidates comprised of houseguests from the first six seasons of Big Brother U.S.. Fan votes trimmed the pool to 14, leaving 6 ineligible to return; producers also selected 6 of the 20 candidates themselves, meaning only 8 houseguests entered based on the public vote.

Big Brother 11 featured 4 past houseguests competing for a single spot to re-enter the game, one aligned with each of the four cliques (The Athletes, The Brains, The Off-Beats, and The Populars). The returning player tied to the clique that won the opening endurance competition moved in as the first Head of Household.

Big Brother 13 featured 6 returning players competing alongside 8 new players. The returnees were paired into duos with partners from their original seasons, with each duo composed of one male and one female.

Big Brother 14 featured 4 returning houseguests who entered as "coaches" mentoring 12 new players (3 per team). Each week, coaches competed in a Coaches Competition to grant immunity to a team member or trade players. In Week 3, the coaches were offered the chance to enter as individual players, which all but one accepted; had they declined, an evicted houseguest would have returned the following week.

Big Brother 18 featured 4 returning players alongside 12 new players. The returnees were placed on separate teams and competed with 3 new houseguests each.

Big Brother: Over The Top featured 2 notable pre-jurors from the previous two seasons placed up for a fan vote to determine the final houseguest of the season.

Big Brother 19 featured the potential return of 1 former fan favorite, contingent on a new houseguest anonymously accepting a temptation of $25,000; the returning player joined only if the money was taken.

Big Brother 22, the second All-Stars season, featured 16 returning houseguests, including winners, finalists, notable personalities, and acclaimed players who had never won, each with something to prove.

The holiday-themed spin-off Big Brother: Reindeer Games featured 9 former players competing across six episodes for a $100,000 grand prize, guided by “Santa’s Elves” rather than Julie Chen-Moonves.

In celebration of the series’ 25th anniversary, Big Brother 27 featured 1 former winner returning to play as part of a twist orchestrated by the season’s narrative villain, "The Mastermind."

Big Brother 28 featured 1 former player coming back as a "Reality Icon" to compete alongside 16 new players, 2 of which are crossing over from Survivor as the other icons.

Big Brother Canada 5 featured 8 returning houseguests facing 8 new players. The game would still be individual, however, with the eight returnees competing alongside the eight newbies.

Big Brother Canada 12 featured 2 returning houseguests facing 12 new players. The returning players were dubbed "Mystery All-Stars" before their identities were revealed.

Ultimate Big Brother was the final season of Big Brother to air on Channel 4 and featured 14 returning housemates from both normal and Celebrity Big Brother.

The nineteenth season of Celebrity Big Brother UK, featured 8 returning celebrity housemates competing alongside 8 new celebrity housemates as a part of that season's All-Stars and New-Stars twist.

The twenty-second season of Big Brother UK, featured 2 housemates that were previously the 1st evicted as late arrivals. 1 was a returning housemate competing alongside the season's new housemate that was originally the 1st evicted and the remaining 12 out of 16 new housemates.

Celebrity Big Brother Australia 1 was the first season in the Australian franchise to feature a returning player, with 1 fan favorite former housemate returning competing alongside 11 other celebrities.

In a similar manner to the previous celebrity season, 1 fan favorite former housemate returned to compete alongside 11 other celebrities.

The fourteenth season of Big Brother Australia featured 8 returning housemates facing off against 14 new housemates. The returning housemates consisted of previous winners, finalists, and other iconic housemates from the franchise's history.

Out of the seven seasons that have featured returning players playing alongside new players, five of them (Big Brother 13, Big Brother 14, Big Brother 18, Big Brother: Over The Top, and Big Brother 19) saw returning players reach the Final Two. These players include Rachel Reilly (Big Brother 13), Dan Gheesling (Big Brother 14), Nicole Franzel (Big Brother 18), Jason Roy (Big Brother: Over The Top), and Paul Abrahamian (Big Brother 19). Rachel and Nicole then went on to win the game, while Dan, Jason, and Paul were the runners-up in their second seasons. Big Brother 11 was the first season to feature a returning player who did not make the Final Two, as Jessie Godderz became the first Jury member. Big Brother 27 would follow suit, with Rachel Reilly returning again and also finishing as the first Juror. Big Brother 28 saw Angela Murray as the sole returnee, and she ended up becoming the Second Juror.

In the two All-Star seasons, Big Brother 7 and Big Brother 22, the entire casts were comprised of returning players. The finalists of Big Brother 7 were Mike Malin and Erika Landin, with Mike winning by a 6-1 vote. The finalists of Big Brother 22 were Cody Calafiore and Enzo Palumbo, with Cody winning by a 9-0 vote. Big Brother: Reindeer Games also featured an all-returnee cast, billed as "BB Legends".

For the remaining returning players who did not make it to the Final Two in their subsequent seasons against new players, their success is as follows: Four returning players were on the Jury on Big Brother 13, with one walking from the game Pre-Jury due to medical reasons. On Big Brother 14, only one was on the Jury, while the other two were evicted Pre-Jury. Big Brother 18 had it reversed, with two of them being on the Jury and only one leaving Pre-Jury.

Six seasons featured former winners coming back for a chance to win the game a second time: Will Kirby on Big Brother 7; Dick Donato and Jordan Lloyd on Big Brother 13; Mike Malin and Dan Gheesling on Big Brother 14; Ian Terry and Nicole Franzel on Big Brother 22; Nicole, Josh Martinez, Cody Calafiore, Xavier Prather and Taylor Hale on Big Brother: Reindeer Games; Rachel Reilly on Big Brother 27.

Of those listed above, at least one former winner made it to at least the Final Four in every season. Dick and Mike were the only two to place Pre-Jury, with Dick having to walk from the game on Big Brother 13, becoming the first one eliminated at 14th place, while Mike was the last Pre-Juror on Big Brother 14, placing 10th. However, Will and Jordan both managed to place 4th on their respective seasons, each serving as the sixth Juror. Ian and Nicole bookended the Jury on Big Brother 22, with Ian placing 11th as the first Juror, while Nicole placed 3rd as the ninth and final Juror. Dan is currently the only one to make it to the Final Two in the main series of Big Brother for an unprecedented second time, where he ultimately lost to Ian in a 6-1 vote on Big Brother 14. In Reindeer Games, Cody was the first winner to be eliminated, placing 8th, followed by Josh who placed 6th. Nicole, Taylor and Xavier all made it to the finale, where Xavier lost the semifinals and placed 3rd, while Nicole and Taylor made it to the Final Two and Nicole took home her second Big Brother victory. Rachel placed 9th and was the first Juror in Big Brother 27.

Lisa Donahue was almost a houseguest on Big Brother 7 alongside Will, but was not voted in by the fans nor chosen to be cast by production, leaving Will as the sole returning winner competing on the first ever All-Stars season.

Dan almost competed on Big Brother 13 alongside Jordan but pulled himself last minute due to conflicts with his wedding; he was replaced by Dick and returned on the following season. Josh and Kaycee Clark were set to compete on Big Brother 22 alongside Ian and Nicole, but were both pulled from the cast at the last minute due to false positive COVID-19 tests, while Hayden Moss was cast as an alternate who ultimately went unused.

During Big Brother Canada 5, Kevin Martin reached the Final Two and went on to win with a Perfect Game.

Despite half of the cast being made up of returning players, three of them still ended up consecutively leaving Pre-Jury, with the remaining four all being on the Jury, although three out of those four left consecutively as well.

Anthony Douglas and Victoria Woghiren returned to compete on Big Brother Canada 12, and while Victoria was evicted in 5th place, Anthony made it to the Final Two for a second time and lost once again, this time in a 6-1 vote.
