# Big Brother 24 (US)

This season introduced a new cast and featured several notable events and twists that shaped the game's dynamics. These included the dissolution of early majority alliances like Old Skool and Po's Pack, the formation and long-term control of The Leftovers alliance, and the introduction of the Festie Bestie twist and split house double eviction. The season also saw Michael Bruner's impressive competition performance, discussions about implicit bias leading to Kyle Capener's eviction, and Taylor Hale's journey from facing mistreatment to ultimately winning the game. Notably, this season crowned the first African-American female winner, who also made history by simultaneously winning America's Favorite Houseguest.

The season was first officially announced by CBS after the airing of the Big Brother 23 (US) finale, where host Julie Chen-Moonves reminded viewers that Celebrity Big Brother 3 (US) was happening as well in the coming winter, after its initial announcement a few weeks earlier.

Backstage Boss: On Night 1, all 16 houseguests would randomly select a ticket to participate in the first Head of Household competition. One of those houseguests would unknowingly pick the "Backstage Boss" pass. This houseguest would be safe from the week and cannot participate in any competitions. After the first HOH was crowned, the Backstage Boss was required to select three houseguests to receive "Backstage Passes". These three would be nominated to potentially leave during the first eviction and would not be able to participate in any competition either. Unknowingly to the rest of the house, America was given the option to save one of the three. On Eviction Night, one of remaining two Backstage houseguests would compete against the houseguest evicted by the house vote in a Hit The Road Competition where the loser would be that night's evictee.
Due to Paloma walking from the game, the twist was cancelled. The competition that would've been the Backstage Duel was incorporated into the first part of the Week 2 HOH competition instead. It has never been confirmed, but it was assumed by many fans that the Backstage Boss would have chosen to save one of the two remaining Backstage houseguests, leaving the last one to compete against the voted out houseguest.
Festie Besties: During the Week 3 Head of Household competition, the houseguests were grouped together in pairs. Similar in format to the Dynamic Duos twist in Big Brother 13 (US), each week the Head of Household would have the sole power nominate one group of "Besties" for eviction while their partner would be immune for the week. For the Power of Veto, one additional group of "Besties" would be chosen to participate. At the end of the week, the group of "Besties" would be divided up with the remaining houseguest able to select any group of "Besties" to join that they want creating a trio, and the possibility for a quartet. In the remaining weeks, if a trio or quartet is nominated, all of the houseguests would be nominated and compete for the Power of Veto ignoring the rule where only six houseguests could compete in after it was introduced in Big Brother 5 (US).
Split House Double Eviction: Before the Week 7 Head of Household competition, Julie announced to the remaining houseguests that the house would be split in half for a Double Eviction to end the week. After the two Heads of Household were crowned the house was split by schoolyard pick into two separate groups of five. Big BroChella will live inside the main house for the entire week while Dyre Fest will be secluded to the backyard only. The two groups would not able to communicate at all and will not see each other until the end of the week when two houseguests, one from each group, will be evicted from the house without getting to say goodbye to anyone from the opposite group. The Dyre Fest group also had to build their own Veto Competition.

Taylor Hale, the eventual winner of the season, faced significant mistreatment from other houseguests early in the game. She was subjected to microaggressions, social isolation, and unfounded accusations. This led to widespread criticism from fans and former houseguests, who called out the behavior as racist and bullying.

Daniel Durston and Nicole Layog in particular faced backlash for their treatment of Taylor, most notably after an incident during the third week where Daniel berated a confused Taylor, blaming her for Paloma Aguilar's voluntary exit from the game while insinuating that she was trying to do the same to Nicole. This outburst was widely condemned by viewers and led to calls for their removal from the show.

Kyle Capener was criticized for suggesting an alliance based on race, proposing to target non-white houseguests. This led to his eventual eviction and a house meeting where his comments were addressed. Kyle later apologized for his actions both in the house and after his eviction.

CBS and the show's producers were criticized for their handling of these issues, with many viewers calling for more direct intervention. In response, CBS released a statement acknowledging the concerns and promising to address them, including providing additional support and training for houseguests.

This is the only season to feature no opening credits.
Taylor Hale later confirmed that a user on Reddit was correct with their guess that it was a network mandate that later got reversed the following season.
Taylor later shared in a Big Brother ChatBCC group that CBS wanted to try it in order to add more show content to the episodes, and since they noticed that the intros often got skipped when episodes were watched on streaming services. She also shared that the houseguests did not even film any poses for potential opening credits.
This season marks the return of the live studio audience during the eviction shows following its absence in the prior two seasons due to the COVID-19 Pandemic. The season started without an audience, but it returned at the first eviction of the jury phase, on Day 44.
Excluding celebrity seasons, this is the first season to feature a tied vote since Big Brother 19 (US).
This is the first season since Big Brother 16 (US) to have multiple houseguests be nominated at least six times.
This is the first season to have two houseguests win four or more Power of Vetos in a single season.
In this case, this was achieved by Michael Bruner and Brittany Hoopes who won 6 and 4 POV's respectively.
However, two of their POV wins were shared between both of them because of the Festie Bestie twist; in total, only 8 POV competitions were won by either of them.
This is the second civilian season in a row to crown an African-American winner, following Big Brother 23 with Xavier Prather,
Coincidentally, both winners are from Michigan.
Paloma is the fourth Big Brother (US) contestant in history to walk from the game.
This is the first season since Big Brother 19 (US) where a contestant walks from the game.
This is the first season to have an African-American male and female in the final 2.
This is the first season in Big Brother US (since the introduction of the Power of Veto) where no female houseguests won both HOH and POV, either together or in separate weeks.
Brittany won 4 POV's but no HOH's, while Jasmine and Taylor won 1 and 2 HOH's respectively, but no POV's.
It would be immediately followed by Big Brother 25.
This is the first season since Big Brother 12 (US) where only one woman won a POV competition.
This is the third season to feature a showmance in the final 2, following Big Brother 7 (US) and Big Brother 21 (US) respectively.
This is the first one in which the woman won.
This is the first season since Big Brother 15 (US) where neither of the final two Houseguests had more than 5 competition wins.
This is the second civilian season to end on a Sunday, following Big Brother 9 (US).
This is the first, and so far only season to have the finale voting and reunion be held in the backyard instead of the stage.
This is the first season where every Houseguest was nominated by the final 9. In every other season, the last houseguest would get nominated at the final 8 or later.
This is the fifth season where all three of the Final 3 Houseguests won multiple HOH competitions, following Big Brother 8 (US), Big Brother 17 (US), Big Brother 19 (US), and Big Brother 21 (US).
This is the first one to have a woman win.
This is the ninth season to have four Houseguests win multiple POV competitions, following Big Brother 9 (US), Big Brother 13 (US), Big Brother 14 (US), Big Brother 16 (US), Big Brother 17 (US), Big Brother 18 (US), Big Brother 20 (US), and Big Brother 23 (US).
This is the second season to have African-American Houseguests win a combined total of 7 HOH's in a season, following Big Brother 23 (US).
It is the second season to feature African-American houseguests winning a combined total of 10 competition wins, following Big Brother 23 (US).
Marvin Achi, who was originally cast on this season before being removed from sequester and replaced with Joseph, would go on to appear on Big Brother Titans 1.
