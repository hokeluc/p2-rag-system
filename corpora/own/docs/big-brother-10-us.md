# Big Brother 10 (US)

Introducing a cast of 13 new houseguests, the season featured a focus on more strategic and social gameplay with fewer twists. Season highlights include the powershift that took out Brian Hart's initial control leading to his eviction, the huge arguments among the houseguests, the infamous Keesha's birthday fight, the week-long America's Player twist, and Dan Gheesling's high-level strategy and maneuvering from the bottom.

After 71 days of unpredictable and high-octane gameplay, Dan Gheesling defeated Memphis Garrett by a vote of 7-0, making the former the first to win a Perfect Game in Big Brother (US) history.

The show premiered July 13, 2008 on the CBS television network,airing three times a week; Sundays at 8 p.m. (ET/PT), Tuesdays at 9 p.m. (ET/PT), and Thursdays at 8 p.m (live ET/delayed PT). Julie Chen returned as host, and Allison Grodner, Rich Meehan and Scott Einziger were the executive producers. The show was produced by Endemol USA and Allison Grodner Productions. The finale was Tuesday, September 16, 2008.

This was the second edition of the show to air in the same year. Originally, only one summer edition was due to air, but the 2007–2008 Writers Guild of America strike prompted CBS to air an additional season, Big Brother 9, from February to April during the regular television season and Big Brother 10 for the summer of 2008.

Big Brother 10 lasted just 71 days, nearly a week and a half shorter than most seasons, making this the shortest season of the American series of Big Brother. This also marked the last time that the season number or year of airing would be mentioned in the broadcast and in the logo; subsequent seasons were simply referred to as Big Brother, with the logo omitting the season number in favor of an animation of a shutting door.

Back to Basics: All of the houseguests were total strangers, unlike previous seasons where some contestants had pre-existing relationships. In addition, live audiences returned for the Thursday eviction episodes, marking the first time this had been done since Season 1.
Temporary America's Player: During Week 4, Dan was offered the chance to become America's Player for one week. He accepted the role, completing all three tasks successfully. America controlled Dan's actions, assigning him tasks for a chance to earn $20,000 at the end of the week.

Before even speaking to one another, the houseguests had to cast their vote for whom they wanted to be the first HOH of the summer. After receiving 4 votes, Jerry MacDonald was crowned HOH. That night, Jessie Godderz and Renny Martyn engaged in an argument after Jessie claimed he couldn't sleep due to Renny being loud. Despite Renny's numerous apologies, Jessie claimed that she did not apologize to him. The following day, Jerry chose to nominate Renny and Jessie for eviction, claiming he wanted to avoid conflict in the house. Jessie won the "Sweet Dreams, Honey" POV competition. The vast majority of the house later went to Jerry and asked him to nominate Brian Hart for eviction, as he had been making deals with numerous houseguests thus far. Jessie used the Veto on himself, and Jerry nominated his former ally Brian. Brian was evicted in a nine to one vote.

Following Brian's eviction, the houseguests competed in the "Majority Rules" Head of Household competition, where Jessie was the winner. Jessie nominated Dan Gheesling and Steven Daigle due to their close friendship with Brian, with Steven as his target, due to feeling disrespected by him.. On Day 11, HouseGuests competed in the "License to Veto" Power of Veto competition, where it was between Michelle Costa and Keesha Smith, but Michelle was the winner. Michelle chose not to use the Power of Veto, despite considering using it on Dan and backdooring Libra Thompson. Steven was evicted from the house in a unanimous vote.

Following Steven's eviction, houseguests competed in the "Alien Abduction" HOH competition. Keesha Smith won the competition and was crowned HOH. She decided to nominate Angie Swindell and Jessie Godderz for eviction. At the "Garden of Veto" competition, Keesha was victorious and won the POV. Despite being asked by Angie and Memphis to use the veto and backdoor Libra Thompson, she finally chose to leave her nominations intact, due to giving Libra her word on Day 4. Angie was evicted unanimously.

At the first endurance competition of the season, "Livin' on the Edge", April Dowling won HOH. In the hope of evicting Memphis Garrett, she decided to nominate Memphis alongside Jessie Godderz. At the "Big Brother Slapshot" POV competition, Libra Thompson was the winner, but chose to take a Hawaiian vacation instead, and gave Jerry MacDonald, the runner-up, the POV for the week. He chose to leave nominations intact. Following numerous arguments in the house, several houseguests turned on Jessie, and in turn, April and Ollie, and decided to go against April's wishes and evict Jessie. Jessie was evicted by a vote of 4 to 3.

At the end of the competition "Rude Awakening", Michelle Costa was the last standing and won HOH. After Michelle won the competition, she swore that her HOH win was "for Jessie". At the nomination ceremony, Michelle revealed that she had decided to nominate Libra Thompson and Keesha Smith, with Libra as her target, despite Jerry campaigning for Dan Gheesling to go on the block. Jerry won the "Cry Me a Veto" POV competition, and chose to leave nominations the same. Libra was evicted unanimously.

Following Libra's eviction, Renny Martyn won the "Diary Room Confessionals" HOH competition. Before Renny made her nominations, the rest of her alliances tried to convince her to nominate both Bryan Ollie and April Dowling together. At the nomination ceremony, Renny decided not to follow her alliance's wishes and nominated Jerry MacDonald alongside April. Dan Gheesling won the "Haunted Yard" Power of Veto Competition and decided not to use his POV. April was evicted by a four to one vote.

After another endurance HOH competition, "King of the Jungle", Dan Gheesling was crowned HOH. During the competition, Dan had made a deal with Bryan Ollie; the deal was that if Ollie let Dan win the competition - he and a houseguest of his choice would be safe from eviction, he would be able to choose one of the nominees and he could choose the replacement nominee if the Veto was used. Ollie asked for Michelle Costa to be safe and for Memphis Garrett to be nominated. Dan originally stuck to the deal and nominated Memphis alongside Jerry MacDonald. However, before the Veto competition, Dan told his alliance that he was not going to stay loyal to the deal. Memphis won the POV competition, "One Giant Leap" and removed himself from the block. Dan chose Michelle as the replacement nominee. Michelle was evicted by a vote of 3 to 1.

The houseguests learned that it would be a Fast Forward night, and a second houseguest would be evicted. Keesha Smith won the "Big Brother Headlines" competition, minutes later, and chose to nominate Jerry MacDonald and Bryan Ollie for eviction. Dan Gheesling later won the "Veto In a Haystack" POV competition, in which houseguests had to dig through hay to find two Veto pieces. He chose to leave Keesha's nominations the same. Minutes later, Ollie was evicted in a unanimous vote.

The HOH competition, "Days Go Bye", was not held until later that night due to the double eviction episode. At the competition, it seemed as if it was Jerry MacDonald versus the rest of the house. However, Jerry managed to win the competition and was crowned HOH. The following day, Jerry nominated Dan Gheesling and Keesha Smith for eviction. Memphis Garrett completed, "Rock a Bye Veto" competition in the fastest time and won the POV. Staying loyal to The Renegades alliance, Memphis decided to use the Veto on Dan. Renny was the only possible replacement nominee, and was evicted unanimously.

After the HOH competition, "Freeze Frame", Dan Gheesling had the most correct answers and became the new HOH. The Renegades came up with a plan to make it look as if they were no longer working together. Following through with the plan, Dan nominated Memphis Garrett and Jerry MacDonald for eviction. The plan was successful as Memphis won the POV competition "Stairway to Veto" and removed himself from the block. Dan and Memphis both decided to turn on their fellow ally Keesha Smith, as they thought she would be more difficult to beat in the final HOH competition than Jerry. Memphis cast the sole vote to evict and chose Keesha.

The final HOH competition is played out in three challenges, to give the remaining houseguests a fair shot at securing a place in the final two. During the first endurance HOH competition, "On A Wing and A Prayer", Dan Gheesling won the challenge. Days later, Memphis Garrett beat Jerry MacDonald in a memory recognition competition, "BB Knockout", meaning Jerry was nominated. At the live HOH part 3 competition, Dan beat Memphis in a personality guessing quiz, "Jury Statements", claiming the final HOH. He then chose to evict Jerry.

This is first season of American Big Brother where the winner won by a Perfect Game (receiving no eviction votes against them and receiving all of the jury votes to win). It would be followed by Big Brother 22 and Big Brother 26.
Big Brother Canada 5's winner Kevin Martin also played a Perfect Game, receiving all 9 jury votes.
Big Brother Canada 7’s winner Dane Rupert played a Perfect Game as well, receiving all 7 jury votes.
Although Celebrity Big Brother 2 (US)'s winner Tamar Braxton did not win with a Perfect Game, she became the second American winner to win unanimously, receiving all 9 jury votes.
This is the first season to have a houseguest in his 70's.
This is the shortest standard-format season of the American Big Brother, lasting 71 days.
This is the first season to have two houseguests over age 50. It would be followed by Big Brother 25 and Big Brother 28.
This is the first season to have a live audience present at every eviction.
This is the first season to feature 2 houseguests with the same first name (Brian Hart and Bryan Ollie). It would be followed by Big Brother 18, Big Brother 20, Big Brother 22, and Big Brother 23.
The latter was referred by his last name throughout the season in order to differentiate between the two.
This season has the most houseguests to go by their nicknames with 3.
This is the third season where women won 4 HOHs in a row, being preceded by Big Brother 5 and Big Brother 8. It was later followed by Big Brother 13, Big Brother 17, Big Brother: Over The Top, Big Brother 20, Big Brother 23 and Big Brother 26.
For this specific season, this occurred weeks 3 through 6.
This is the second season to have an all male final three, after Big Brother 1. It was followed by Big Brother 12.
